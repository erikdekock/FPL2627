## 1. Confirmed 2026/27 ruleset (official, 20 July)

**Chips — unchanged from last season.** Two sets of four: Wildcard, Free Hit, Triple Captain, Bench Boost, once each per half = **8 chips total**. First set must be played before the **GW19 deadline (13:30 GMT, Sat 2 Jan 2027)** and cannot be carried over. **Only one chip per Gameweek.** Free Hit **cannot** be played in GW1, and using FH1 in GW19 blocks FH2 in GW20.

**Transfers.** Still 1 free transfer per GW, rollable up to 5. **No extra December free transfers this season** (no AFCON in 2026/27) — last season's December top-up does not repeat.

**Defensive contribution (DefCon) points stay.**

**BPS tweaked** to reduce overlap with DefCon — explicitly aimed at improving bonus prospects for **goalkeepers, full-backs and attacking players**. ⚠️ Strategic implication to test: the "cheap centre-back who racks up DefCon *and* bonus" archetype loses part of its bonus edge; attacking returns get relatively more bonus.

**Later lockdown.** Scores now final at **09:00 UK on the day after the Gameweek's last match** (previously one hour after the final whistle), so post-match Opta review can still change BPS and DefCon. ⚠️ Practical: full-time points are provisional. **Do not review a Gameweek before lockdown** — our review session moves to after 09:00 UK the following morning.

**New live features.** Global rank, mini-league standings, H2H and FPL Cup ties update in real time; projected bonus appears after 20 minutes of each match.

**Official early chip anchors** (from the Premier League's own chip guide — first half of the season): Haaland at home to promoted sides in **GW3 (Coventry)**, **GW7 (Ipswich)** and **GW16 (Hull)**; Fernandes at home to **Ipswich in GW2** and **Coventry in GW14**. The official line also floats Bench Boost in GW1 or immediately after a Wildcard.

**Still unknown:** position reclassifications, price list, any further features. → Phase 1.

## 5. Where the edge actually is this season

1. **Patience arbitrage.** Eight new managers plus World Cup fatigue means the field will overreact to GW1–3 noise. A squad built to survive uncertainty — nailed minutes, no speculative premiums at churn clubs — beats a squad built to win week one.
2. **Fatigue asymmetry.** Deep World Cup runners return in early August with no pre-season. Their price tags assume last season's output. Everyone knows this vaguely; almost nobody maps it player by player.
3. **The BPS re-write.** Bonus is being deliberately shifted away from DefCon-heavy defenders toward keepers, full-backs and attackers. If the price list still prices the 25/26 archetype, that gap is exploitable in the first weeks.
4. **Reclassification and price-list day-one scanning.** A systematic sweep on launch day beats a week of vibes.
5. **Chip discipline.** Four chips expire on 2 January whether or not they are used. A written plan with dated windows beats reactive firing — and the official anchors give first-half Triple Captain a concrete shape already.
6. **Process compounding.** A logged decision every week means by December we know which of our reasoning patterns actually earns points.
